Morocco's Bill 52-26: what it could mean for private practices

A bill before Parliament plans a shared medical record for every patient, kept up to date by health professionals. It is not law yet. Here is what has been reported, what already applies today, and what a practice can do now.

26 Nov 20264 min read

This article is general information, not legal advice. The bill can still change before it is adopted. For your own situation, ask a lawyer or your professional body.

What the bill plans

Bill 52-26 concerns Morocco's national integrated health information system. According to Bladi.net, it was filed with the House of Representatives on 31 July 2026. 1

As reported, the bill would:

  • create a shared medical record for each patient, which health professionals must update after each consultation or medical act; 1
  • give health facilities up to 24 months to connect their IT systems, counted from the publication of the technical rules; 1
  • fine a professional or facility head who fails to update the record between 50,000 and 300,000 DH, with the same range for a facility that does not connect its IT system; 1
  • fine illegal access to a shared record up to 200,000 DH. 1

Other press reports also describe a national health identifier for each patient. 2 3

What is not settled

The bill is not law. Its final text, its dates and its fines may still change in Parliament. The 24-month period would only start once the technical rules are published, and those rules will say how a practice's software has to connect.

Until then, no one can honestly say that a program is compliant with Bill 52-26. What a practice can do is get its records into a shape that makes connecting easier when the rules arrive: digital, complete and well protected.

What already applies: Law 09-08 and the CNDP

While Bill 52-26 is still a bill, Law 09-08 on the protection of personal data dates from 2009 and applies today. It counts data relating to health as sensitive data. 4 Two of its articles matter most for a practice.

  • Article 57: processing health data without the express consent of the persons concerned can lead to 6 months to 2 years in prison and a fine of 50,000 to 300,000 DH, or one of the two. 4
  • Article 58: processing personal data without the security measures required by Articles 23 and 24 can lead to 3 months to 1 year in prison and a fine of 20,000 to 200,000 DH, or one of the two. 4

The CNDP, Morocco's national commission for the protection of personal data, oversees how the law is applied. It is the place to ask which declaration or authorisation your processing of patient data needs.

Data breaches do happen in Morocco. In April 2025, a cyberattack on the National Social Security Fund (CNSS) allegedly exposed information on nearly 2 million people, according to Moroccan media reports. 5

The wider picture

More patients now have health insurance. Generalising compulsory health insurance (AMO) covered more than 32 million beneficiaries in 2025, 88% of the population, up from 42% before generalisation. 6 More insured patients also means more claims paperwork for practices.

Public investment in digital is growing too. The Head of Government said it rose from 11 million DH in 2021 to more than 1.7 billion DH in 2024. 7

For dentists, the scale is clear. The 2025 health map (carte sanitaire) of the Ministry of Health and Social Protection counts 6,552 private dental practices, as reported by Aujourd'hui le Maroc. 8 If the bill passes as reported, each of them would have to keep a shared record up to date after every consultation.

What a practice can do now

  1. List every place your patient data lives today: paper cards, spreadsheets, phones, an old program.
  2. Ask for the patient's consent to process their health data, and keep a record of it.
  3. Decide who in the practice may see which part of a patient's file, and make sure your tools respect it.
  4. Protect what you already keep: computers locked when unattended, data encrypted, and backups that run on their own and are kept away from the computer.
  5. Ask the CNDP which declaration or authorisation applies to your practice.
  6. Move active patient records to software that encrypts data, controls access by role and keeps a log of who did what.
  7. When the technical rules are published, ask your software vendor how and when they will connect to the shared record.

None of these steps depends on the final text of the bill. Each one is useful under Law 09-08 today.
