04

One secure core under every MeDoc program

We build the protection once, in a core that every MeDoc program shares. Each new program starts with the same safeguards instead of rebuilding them.

  • Encryption at rest

    The practice database and every backup are encrypted on the disk where they are stored.

  • Roles enforced in the core

    What each account may see and do is checked by the core itself, not only hidden on the screen.

  • Tamper-evident audit chain

    Changes and reads of medical records go into a log. Each entry is chained to the one before, so an altered or deleted entry is detected.

  • Sync over the local network

    The practice's computers share one encrypted database over the local network, with each change signed. In MeDoc Dentist, this comes with Cabinet and Cabinet+.

  • Signed licences

    Each licence carries our digital signature, and the program checks it, so a forged or altered licence is refused.

  • Automatic encrypted backups

    Once a day the core makes a compressed, encrypted backup by itself, and removes old copies on a fixed rotation so the disk does not fill up.

07

Why we started MeDoc with dentists

The patient card shown above is real. It comes from a Moroccan dental practice. On one sheet of paper it holds a printed tooth chart, the treatments written by hand, the prices and what has been paid. Cards like this one are why we started with dentists.

Paper has only one copy. A card can be misfiled, damaged or lost, and handwriting can be misread. When a university dental hospital in South Africa audited its records, 105 of the 362 files it wanted to review could not be found.5

The rules are changing too. Bill 52-26, filed with Parliament in July 2026 and not yet law, would give every patient a shared medical record that professionals update after each consultation.4 A card in a drawer cannot take part in that.

We decided that patient data should stay in the practice. MeDoc runs on the practice's own computers and works without the internet, so a cut connection does not stop the day. Data kept in the practice has to be protected there, so the database and its backups are encrypted, and every reading of a medical record is logged.

Health and social data are a real target. Moroccan media reported that the April 2025 cyberattack on the national social security fund (CNSS) exposed information on nearly 2 million people.6

Software alone does not take a practice off paper. Someone has to look at how the practice works, bring the existing lists in, train the people who use it every day and answer the first questions. That is why every MeDoc program comes with the Digitalization Pack, and why the people who build MeDoc are the ones who help you switch.
